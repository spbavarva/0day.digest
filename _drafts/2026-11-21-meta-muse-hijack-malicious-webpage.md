Researchers showed that a single malicious webpage could extract a Meta
Muse user's private conversations and stored memories without triggering
any approval prompt.

The same technique could reportedly be extended to reach credentials Muse
stores for a user's connected accounts.

The work was a laboratory demonstration rather than an attack on real
users, and stayed within the scope of the researcher's own account.
