Security firm Claroc found 37,571 apps built with Grok's AI app-building
feature exposed online, of which 35,578 were accessible without any
sign-in. About one in ten of the open apps contained internal business
data or passwords.

The exposed apps included 24 built by staff at X and SpaceX, the companies
behind the Grok assistant. Most open apps were low-risk games, landing
pages, and calculators, but the exposure shows how AI-generated apps can
ship without basic access controls by default.

Anyone who used Grok to build an app should verify it isn't publicly
accessible if it handles sensitive data.
