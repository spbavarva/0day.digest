Researchers at eSentire disclosed a campaign in which a threat actor used a
Microsoft Store application to deliver the Vinci remote access trojan.
Victims were redirected to a bogus site impersonating a game-sniping tool,
where a Microsoft-signed installer named "Snipping-Tool.exe" delivered the
RAT.

Vinci RAT gives attackers remote control over compromised systems. The
campaign illustrates how attackers abuse trusted distribution channels,
like the Microsoft Store, to bypass user suspicion of malicious installers.
