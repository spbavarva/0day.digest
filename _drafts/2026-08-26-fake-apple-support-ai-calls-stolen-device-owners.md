Researchers disclosed a phishing-as-a-service platform, tracked as
AnonyMousKIT, that uses rented AI voice agents posing as Apple Support
to call theft victims and ask for their device passcode. The goal is to
strip Apple's Activation Lock from stolen devices so they can be
resold.

The credit-metered platform automates a social-engineering step that
previously required a live human caller, lowering the cost and skill
needed to run this kind of scam at scale.
