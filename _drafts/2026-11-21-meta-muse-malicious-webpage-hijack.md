Researchers demonstrated that a single malicious webpage could extract a
Meta Muse user's private conversations and stored memories without
triggering any approval prompt. They said the same technique could be
extended to reach credentials Muse stores for the user's connected
accounts.

The work was a laboratory demonstration against the researchers' own
account, not an attack on real users. It highlights the risk of
browsing-capable AI assistants processing untrusted web content without
strong isolation from assistant memory and stored credentials.
