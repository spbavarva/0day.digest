An analysis piece traces phishing's evolution from bad content (scan and
block malicious payloads), to bad intent (social-engineering messages
without a malicious payload), to what it calls "Phishing 3.0" — attacks
where the sender itself is an AI agent rather than a person. The argument
is that traditional scan-and-block email defenses lose effectiveness once
both attack generation and evasion are automated, pushing defenders toward
AI-based detection of intent rather than content.
