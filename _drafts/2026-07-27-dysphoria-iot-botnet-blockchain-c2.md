Dysphoria, an IoT botnet tracked by CNCERT and XLab, has adopted
blockchain-based name services and infected-device relays for its
command-and-control infrastructure. The upgrade follows a March
law-enforcement operation that disrupted JackSkid, the botnet's predecessor
infrastructure. By distributing C2 resolution and relay functions across the
blockchain and compromised devices themselves, the operators aim to make the
botnet harder to take down. The evolution reflects a broader trend of botnet
operators building takedown resistance directly into their infrastructure.
