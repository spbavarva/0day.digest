Researchers say the iAuthFlow V2 phishing toolkit can register an
attacker-controlled passkey during a compromised session, giving persistent
account access that survives password resets and active session
revocation. Because passkeys are treated as a stronger auth factor,
victims and defenders may not think to check for rogue passkey
registrations during incident cleanup.
