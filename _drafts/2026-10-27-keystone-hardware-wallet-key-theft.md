An attacker who compromised Keystone's website obtained the cryptographic
key the company's hardware wallets use to prove they're genuine. With
that key, an attacker could theoretically build counterfeit Keystone
devices that pass the official authenticity check, though Keystone says
the incident did not involve device firmware or seed-generation
compromise.

Keystone is destroying 18,835 hardware wallets as a precaution. Buyers
should verify device authenticity through channels beyond the
compromised verification key until Keystone confirms a replacement
mechanism.
