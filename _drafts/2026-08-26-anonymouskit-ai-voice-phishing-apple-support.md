Researchers disclosed a phishing-as-a-service platform, tracked as
AnonyMousKIT, that strips Apple's Activation Lock from stolen devices using
rented AI voice agents. The bots call theft victims posing as Apple Support
and ask them to hand over their device passcode and 2FA codes.

SOCRadar's Threat Research Unit says the platform is credit-metered and
drives lures across multiple channels. It illustrates how AI voice synthesis
is lowering the bar for convincing vishing campaigns at scale.
